01 · Setting the Stage

Onboarding is where a stranger decides whether to trust you with their health

Most diet apps lose the user before the plan loads. Pantry’s first-run onboarding is built for that exact moment, where a stranger decides whether to hand over their body, habits, and history.

Competitors pick one: a warm intro that collects nothing, or a clinical form that connects with no one. Pantry does both.

The goal: a personalized plan the user believes in, and enjoys reaching.
02 · The Core Problem

Diet apps are great at data, bad at change

People don’t quit health apps over wrong macros. They quit for behavioral reasons:

✕
All mind, no behavior change
✕
One-size-fits-all plans
✕
Restriction → rebound (the yo-yo)
✕
Long, transactional questionnaires
✕
No context: work, tools, social life

The root issue: onboarding asks a lot and returns a spreadsheet, never the why, then labels foods “bad,” priming the restrict-and-rebound cycle that makes people quit.

Diet apps today
Collect data, then generate a plan
One plan for every body and schedule
Foods are “good” or “bad”
Motivation by pressure & guilt

03 · Research · Competitive Teardown

I onboarded through every major player and stole the good parts

I onboarded through six competitors, keeping what worked and flagging the traps.

✓
Noom · Keep
Beginner-friendly, education-first, quiz that predicts your date. But: endless screens, light on doing.
✓
CalAI · Keep
Short & sweet, motivating weight graph, clean unit toggles. But: “lose 2×” claims feel salesy.
✓
Macrofactor · Keep
“Diet sidekick” tone, science-forward, motivating estimate reveal. But: assumes an expert user.
✕
Welling.ai · Avoid
Nice plan-ready reveal with chart. But: screening-heavy, clinical, and long.
✕
Cleaneats · Avoid
Strong “hidden sugar” hook. But: fear-based framing, overwhelming question count.
◇
Aavia · Inspo
Playful branding + in-app UI inspiration. Onboarding stays simple; richness lives in the app.
04 · What the Teardown Taught Me

Four insights that shaped every screen

01
Don’t assume expertise
Most flows assume you already track macros. Meeting beginners where they are turns friction into confidence.
02
Friction ≠ commitment
Typing everything deepens commitment but hurts retention. Suggested responses are the middle ground.
03
Motivation needs proof
Value screens (your macros, your savings vs. takeout, your future self) earn the right to charge.
04
Real life > diet rules
Schedules, kitchens, Friday-night pizza: treat them as inputs, not edge cases, and the plan sticks.
05 · Design Principles

How Pantry’s onboarding is different

✓
CBT inserts. Micro-lessons between question sets reframe the mindset toward progress, not restriction.
✓
Value screens. Recurring “mini-aha” moments prove value before the paywall.
✓
Avi, the companion. One avocado character carries the form into a 24/7 relationship.
✓
Two levels of focus. Selected options outlined, CTA filled: hierarchy without extra color.
✓
Adaptive flow. Later screens and pep-talks adapt to earlier answers.
CBT micro-lessons explain the why behind each recommendation. Structured systems produce ~2× more sustained change: structure, not willpower.

09 · Beyond Onboarding · The Daily Product

Where onboarding ends, the conversation begins

Onboarding earns trust; the daily product keeps it. Snap a photo, ask a question, check in, and Avi handles the rest. I rebuilt the core loop against current conversational-AI patterns, so day 30 feels as considered as minute one.

The patterns I designed against
Capability transparency
Prompts and nudges show what Avi can do. No blank-box paralysis.
Confidence & control
Estimates are labeled and editable. Avi never fakes certainty.
Structured replies
Answers come as cards and quick-replies, not walls of text.
Short, human turns
Messages stay short and split into beats for one-handed reading.
Sourced insight
Trends cite their source (“18 meals this week”), so numbers feel earned, not magic.
One calm action
One obvious next step per screen; quiet palette, never clinical.

10 · The Strategy Behind the Flow

Personalization earns the paywall

Trust curve

Warmth up front reduces friction at every later ask

  • Human tone from screen one
  • Name + greeting reciprocity
  • Explain the “why” behind each ask
Value before price

Prove the system before charging for it

  • Editable macro targets
  • Savings vs. takeout
  • A personalized week they can picture
Objection handling

Answer every past reason for quitting

  • Adaptive CBT inserts
  • Reframed in the user’s own words
  • Peak emotion right before the ask
7  CBT inserts 3+  value screens 6  phases of effort ↔ reward 2×  more likely to sustain change
11 · Reflection

What I’d test & do next

  • Length vs. depth. 39 screens is rich; A/B a “core” path that defers texture, social & work-setup screens to post-onboarding via Avi.
  • Beginner vs. expert forks. Detect knowledge level and adapt question phrasing, à la Noom.
  • Instrument the drop-off. Measure completion by phase and per-CBT-insert to validate the “ask-then-reward” pacing.
  • Commitment vs. friction. Test typed answers vs. suggested chips on retention, not just completion.
Open questions
  • Is cuisine-ranking too much for onboarding, or a delight worth keeping?
  • How much health literacy do we assume, and where does Avi teach instead of ask?
  • Which “aha” screen actually moves conversion the most?
12 · What This Work Represents

Onboarding is behavior design, not a form

Health apps don’t fail on tracking. They fail because onboarding never earns trust. Pantry is my approach to behavior-change design:

01
Understand the person before the plan
02
Prove value before the paywall
03
Flexible by design, nothing off-limits
04
Human and warm, never clinical
Prove you understand someone (their life, their taste, their history) before you ask for anything.
That’s how a stranger becomes a believer, and a believer becomes a daily habit.
